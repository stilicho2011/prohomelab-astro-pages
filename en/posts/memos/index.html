    Memos - Setting Up Self-Hosted Notes for Quick Thoughts

    If you don't need a full-blown PKM system like Obsidian or Trilium, and just want to jot down a thought before you lose it, Memos fills that exact gap. It's basically a self-hosted Google Keep: lightweight, no bloated interface, but with your own server under the hood and full control over your data.

    I go through it in the video and do a full write-up alongside it - what it is, who it's for, and how to get it running in Docker.

    What Memos is

    Memos is an open-source note-taking app built around a timeline. No page hierarchy, no databases inside notes, no block structures like you'd get in Notion or Obsidian - just a chronological feed of short entries written in Markdown, with tags right in the text and fast search.

    If Obsidian or Trilium is a full-on knowledge base, Memos is closer to a technical microblog for yourself: drop a thought, slap a tag on it, move on.

    Who it's for

    Memos makes sense if you don't need nested structures, links between notes, kanban boards, or full wiki logic.

    What you do need is a fast way to capture a thought, a project idea, or a quick technical note without opening a heavyweight PKM app. It's essentially a self-hosted alternative to Google Keep or Apple Notes, except your notes stay on your own server instead of flying off to someone else's.

    Timeline and tags

    Every entry lands in a single chronological feed - handy for jotting down ideas as you work through a project. Tags (#docker, #proxmox, #traefik) let you filter down to what you need later without scrolling through everything.

    Multiple users

    You can set up separate users with isolated data and roles - useful if you want one instance for the whole family or a small team instead of spinning up a Memos instance for each person.

    Minimal requirements

    No external PostgreSQL, no heavy dependencies, no complicated config out of the box - by default Memos runs on SQLite. That makes it a solid candidate for a mini PC, a single-board computer, or a small VPS where you have no reason to run anything heavier.

    A couple of nuances if you're building this out for your own domain:

    • ports is commented out on purpose - the container doesn't publish a port directly, all traffic comes in through Traefik over the proxy network.
    • Instead of MEMOS_MODE=prod, which shows up in older examples, I used MEMOS_INSTANCE_URL - the current variable from Memos' documentation that sets the instance's canonical external address. It matters specifically when running behind a reverse proxy: without it, links to shared notes can end up generated incorrectly.
    • Port 5230 here isn't the default (recent versions of Memos listen on 8081 if no port is set explicitly), but since it's spelled out in both MEMOS_PORT and the Traefik label, everything lines up.

    Conclusion

    If Obsidian or Trilium feels like overkill for what's essentially a list of quick notes, Memos fills exactly that gap: self-hosted, lightweight, Markdown and tags, running in five minutes. It won't become a full knowledge base, but as a self-hosted stand-in for Google Keep, it does the job perfectly.
